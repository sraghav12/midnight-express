How to play

Midnight Express

Everyone in the room drives a train. There isn't enough track. Get home before midnight.

  1. 1

    Board

    Type a name, tap Board the train. You get a train, a destination and 100 tokens. The run starts a few seconds after the first player joins — you can also join one already going.

  2. 2

    Pick your track

    Your train moves on its own. Before each junction, tap the track you want next. Fastest is the shortest way home; Occupied means someone's on it. You can choose while still moving.

  3. 3

    When your phone takes over — bid

    Two trains want the same track. You have 8 seconds. Drag the slider or tap 25% · 50% · All in. Bids are sealed — nobody sees yours. Pass (or not answering) bids zero.

  4. 4

    Highest bid goes first…

    …but pays the second-highest price. So bidding what the track is really worth to you is always your best move. If you lose, you wait — and the winner's payment is split among the losers.

  5. 5

    Get home

    When everyone's in, the board shows how the room did against one central dispatcher with full information. Then tap View this run on Solana — every auction you were in is a real transaction your train signed.

The big screen

Reading the dispatch board

Four tracks contested at once, one second after departure. Tap to enlarge.

  • Stations are the brass rings — Pittsburgh neighbourhoods. The white clock top-right counts up from departure; a run lasts 3 minutes, or ends early when everyone's home.
  • Grey track has a train on it. Dark track is free.
  • Red, pulsing track is being auctioned right now. The chip above it says who's bidding.
  • Big dots with a name are players — your colour matches your row in the Trains list. Small unlabelled dots are freight and AI trains.
  • Red ring around a train: held at a signal, waiting for the track to clear.
  • Green ring: home.
  • The purple train is the AI rival, named after the model driving it (K2 today). Same rules, same budget.
  • Bottom line is the dispatcher's PA — also spoken aloud. It calls every contest, every settlement and every arrival.

Right column: the QR to join · Contested track — every live auction with its countdown · Trains — everyone's token balance. A red edge means that train is bidding or held; green means home; faded rows aren't human.

Your phone

Three screens, in order

Driving. Your route and tokens up top. Tap a track to choose it. Hold the bottom button to run; let go to coast.

Bidding. The red bar is your 8 seconds. Slide, or tap a preset. It says who else wants the track.

Arrived (or Stranded at midnight). Your delay, auctions, tokens, and the link to your bids on Solana.

Coasting is the quiet move: your train runs at full speed until you touch Hold to run. After that, hold to run and let go to crawl — slower, and it counts as delay, but sometimes cheaper than paying for a contested track.

When the run ends

The scoreboard

Left replay: what you all did. Right replay: the same trains, same starts, routed by one dispatcher who knows everything.

Total delay, all trains adds up every second every train spent held or coasting. The big line is the verdict — how many times slower the room was than a single dispatcher. Below it, how the AI rival did against the average human, and a short recap written live by Grok.
